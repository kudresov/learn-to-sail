The rules, in one breath

Arcs · Rule 21

Masthead light: white, 225°, from right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on each side. Sidelights: green to starboard, red to port, 112.5° each, right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam. Sternlight: white, 135°, the rest. Masthead plus sidelights therefore covers exactly what the sternlight does not: see a low white alone and you are in her overtaking sector.

Read her in three looks

1. The stack (all-round lights in a vertical line) names her. 2. Mastheads say power-driven, and how long. 3. Sidelights say which way, and whether she is making way at all.

Sail and power · Rules 23, 25

Sidelights and sternlight with no masthead light: sail. Under 20 m she may combine them in one tricolour at the masthead. Optional red over green all-round at the masthead, never with a tricolour. Motor-sailing: power-driven lights, cone point down by day. Power under 12 m: one all-round white plus sidelights. Under 7 m and under 7 knots: all-round white, plus sidelights if practicable. Sailing under 7 m, or under oars: the same lights if practicable, otherwise a torch ready to show in time.

Underway, making way, at anchor

Underway means not anchored, moored or aground, even if stopped. Making way means moving through the water. Masthead lights go with underway (power, towing, constrained by draught). Sidelights on a hampered vessel (fishing, NUC, RAM) go with making way. A pilot vessel keeps her sidelights whenever underway. At anchor, a restricted vessel adds anchor lights; a fishing vessel never does. Under 7 m at anchor clear of channels and anchorages, no light needed; under 12 m aground, no aground lights needed.

Risk of collision · Rule 7

Hand-bearing compass on her lights, then again a minute or two later, at least three times. Steady bearing and closing range: collision course. Bearing drawing forward, toward your bow: she passes ahead. Drawing aft: she passes astern. Big ships and tows can still hit you with a slowly changing bearing.
